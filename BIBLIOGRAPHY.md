## 1. Foundational Books & Grammars

### The Tai Languages of Assam: A Grammar and Texts (2005)
- **Authors**: Stephen Morey
- **Publisher**: Pacific Linguistics, Research School of Pacific and Asian Studies, The Australian National University
- **ISBN**: `0-85883-549-9`
- **Access URL**: [https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/146747](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/146747)
- **Summary**: Foundational grammar and documented corpus of the Tai languages of Assam (Khamyang, Aiton, Phake, Khamti, and Ahom). Accompanied by an extensive digital corpus of interlinear texts, audio recordings, and linguistic analyses collected in Powaimukh.

### Linguistic Survey of India, Vol. II: Mon-Khmer and Siamese-Chinese Families (including Khassi and Tai) (1904)
- **Authors**: George Abraham Grierson
- **Publisher**: Office of the Superintendent of Government Printing, India
- **Access URL**: [https://archive.org/details/linguisticsurvey02grie](https://archive.org/details/linguisticsurvey02grie)
- **Summary**: Historical documentation of the 'Nora' (Tai Khamyang) language in Upper Assam. Contains 19th-century wordlists, standard translations of the Parable of the Prodigal Son, and grammatical sketches.

### The Tai-Kadai Languages (2008)
- **Authors**: Anthony V. N. Diller, Jerold A. Edmondson, Yongxian Luo
- **Publisher**: Routledge
- **ISBN**: `978-0-7007-1457-5`
- **Summary**: Standard authoritative reference on the Tai-Kadai linguistic phylum. Contains comparative chapters on Southwestern Tai phonological evolution and the northern branch varieties spoken in Assam.

### Tai of North-East India: A Study in Cultural Continuity and Change (1996)
- **Authors**: Puspa Gogoi
- **Publisher**: Chumphra Printers & Publishers
- **Summary**: In-depth anthropological and ethnolinguistic study of the Tai diaspora communities of Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, documenting Buddhist festivals, Lik Tai scripts, and costume traditions.

### The Khamyangs of Assam: A Sociolinguistic Profile (1998)
- **Authors**: Yehom Buragohain
- **Publisher**: Anundoram Borooah Institute of Language, Art and Culture (ABILAC)
- **Summary**: Detailed sociological monograph assessing language transmission within Powaimukh families, bilingualism with Assamese, and traditional social institutions.

### Ahom Lexicon (Based on Original Tai Manuscripts) (1964)
- **Authors**: B. K. Barua, N. N. Deori
- **Publisher**: Department of Historical and Antiquarian Studies, Government of Assam
- **Access URL**: [https://archive.org/details/AhomLexicon](https://archive.org/details/AhomLexicon)
- **Summary**: Classical lexical compilation derived from historical Tai Ahom manuscripts with extensive comparative cognate listings for Nora (Khamyang), Khamti, and Phake.

## 2. Research Articles & Chapters

### Tonal change in the Tai languages of Northeast India (2008)
- **Authors**: Stephen Morey
- **Journal**: *Linguistics of the Tibeto-Burman Area* (Vol. 31, pp. 139-165)
- **Direct Link**: [https://sealang.net/sala/archives/pdf8/morey2008tonal.pdf](https://sealang.net/sala/archives/pdf8/morey2008tonal.pdf)
- **Summary**: Detailed acoustic and historical analysis of tonal splits and mergers in Tai Khamyang, Tai Phake, and Tai Aiton using the Gedney tone box model. Demonstrates the contrastive 6-tone inventory of Khamyang.

### Oral literature of the Tai languages of Assam: A field linguist's perspective (2014)
- **Authors**: Stephen Morey
- **Book**: *Oral Literature in the Digital Age: Archiving Orality and Connecting with Communities*
- **Direct Link**: [https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0032](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0032)
- **Summary**: Examines the oral genres of the Tai communities of Assam, with focus on traditional songs (fɯn), folklore, ritual chants, and community collaboration with Powaimukh speakers.

### Language Endangerment and Revitalisation: The Case of Tai Khamyang in Assam (2013)
- **Authors**: Palash Kumar Nath
- **Journal**: *Journal of Indian Folkloristics* (Vol. 14, pp. 45-62)
- **Direct Link**: [https://gauhati.ac.in/member/palash-kumar-nath](https://gauhati.ac.in/member/palash-kumar-nath)
- **Summary**: Socio-linguistic assessment of language vitality, language shift to Assamese, and revitalization efforts in Powaimukh village. Discusses mother-tongue education initiatives.

### Documentation of the Tai Khamyang Language: Role of Digital Humanities (2017)
- **Authors**: Palash Kumar Nath
- **Journal**: *Journal of the Department of Humanities and Social Sciences* (Vol. 8, pp. 88-104)
- **Summary**: Explores methodologies in digitizing palm-leaf manuscripts (Lik), oral storytelling, and establishing community archives for the Nora/Khamyang community.

### The Tai-Khamyangs of Assam: Their History and Culture (1992)
- **Authors**: J. N. Phukan
- **Journal**: *The Tai Culture* (Vol. 1, pp. 34-48)
- **Summary**: Historical tracing of the Nora kingdom, chronicles of migration across the Patkai passes, settlement along the Disang river, and the founding of Powaimukh village.

## 3. Digital Repositories & Audio Archives

### 🎙️ Documentation of the Oral Literature of the Tai Khamyang Community in Upper Assam, India (2015)
- **Collector/PI**: Palash Kumar Nath
- **Archive**: Endangered Languages Archive (ELAR), SOAS University of London
- **Archive Repository URI**: [https://elar.soas.ac.uk/Collection/MPI1029884](https://elar.soas.ac.uk/Collection/MPI1029884)
- **Scope**: Major digital collection funded by the Endangered Languages Documentation Programme (ELDP). Features hours of high-fidelity audio/video recordings of Khamyang oral tales, song performances, ritual chants, and conversation in Powaimukh.

### 🎙️ Tai and Tibeto-Burman languages of Assam (PARADISEC Collection SDM03) (2005)
- **Collector/PI**: Stephen Morey
- **Archive**: Pacific and Regional Archive for Digital Sources in Endangered Cultures (PARADISEC)
- **Archive Repository URI**: [https://catalog.paradisec.org.au/collections/SDM03](https://catalog.paradisec.org.au/collections/SDM03)
- **Scope**: Comprehensive primary field recording archive containing Tai Khamyang audio tracks from 2000-2005. Includes the 2000 Powaimukh wedding ceremony, fortune-telling chants, war recollections by Chaw Sa Myat Chowlik, and grammatical elicitations.

### 🎙️ Further recordings of Tai languages of Assam: Song Kham Soi Yoi (SDM07-2007-001) (2007)
- **Collector/PI**: Stephen Morey, Ai Che
- **Archive**: Pacific and Regional Archive for Digital Sources in Endangered Cultures (PARADISEC)
- **Archive Repository URI**: [https://catalog.paradisec.org.au/repository/SDM07/2007-001](https://catalog.paradisec.org.au/repository/SDM07/2007-001)
- **Scope**: Recording of traditional Tai Khamyang courting song 'Kham Soi Yoi' practiced and sung with singer Ai Che in Powaimukh.

## 4. Online Dictionaries & Portals

### 🌐 SEAlang Library: Tai and Tibeto-Burman Languages of Assam
- **Institution**: SEAlang Projects
- **Portal**: [http://sealang.net/assam/](http://sealang.net/assam/)
- **Description**: Interactive online database providing searchable interlinear glossed texts, lexicon dictionaries, concordances, and audio playback for Tai Khamyang and neighboring varieties.

### 🌐 Glottolog 5.0 Language: Khamyang (kham1282)
- **Institution**: Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology
- **Portal**: [https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/kham1282](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/kham1282)
- **Description**: Comprehensive bibliographic repository, linguistic tree classification, and geographical mapping for Tai Khamyang (ISO 639-3: ksu).

### 🌐 Centre for Endangered Languages (CFEL) Tai Khamyang Documentation
- **Institution**: Tezpur University
- **Portal**: [http://www.tezu.ernet.in/cfel/](http://www.tezu.ernet.in/cfel/)
- **Description**: Research repository and pedagogical documentation conducted by Tezpur University field teams under UGC programs in Powaimukh.
