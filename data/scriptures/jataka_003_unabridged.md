# 📜 Serivanija Jataka: The Honest Hawker and the Golden Bowl (Complete Story)
**Tai Khamyang**: *Lik4 Caat4 003: Serivanija5 Caat4 (Unabridged)*
**Genre**: `buddhist_jataka_complete` | **Informant/Reciter**: Chaw Sa Myat Chowlik
**Location**: Powaimukh, Assam

> Complete unabridged edition of the classic Buddhist tale of the Golden Bowl, contrasting honest Seriva (the Bodhisatta) with the greedy peddler (Devadatta).

## Full Canonical Verses & Sentences


### Serivanija Jataka (Unabridged)

**[jataka_003_s01]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၽႇ သဝ် ယုႉ ꩢႉ တႃၼ်ႈ ꩢႉ ဢတ်း
- **Romanized**: `phra2 chaw1 yu5 savatthi5 taan3 serivanija5 caat4`
- **Assamese Script**: ফ² ছাৱ¹ যু⁵ স⁵ তান³ স⁵ অত্‌⁴
- **Translation**: *"The Blessed One told the story of Serivanija concerning a monk who gave up diligent effort."*

**[jataka_003_s02]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၼႆႊ မုင် ꩢႉ မိ ၽုႉ ၶႆ ၶူင် ꩢူင် ၵုၼ်
- **Romanized**: `nai6 mung1 seri5 mi1 phu5 khai1 khong1 song1 kun1`
- **Assamese Script**: নাই⁶ মুঙ¹ স⁵ মি¹ ফু⁵ খাই¹ খোঙ¹ সোঙ¹ কুন¹
- **Translation**: *"In the city of Seri, there were two peddlers of pots and brass pans who journeyed from town to town."*

**[jataka_003_s03]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ေမး ထဝ်ႈ ၵပ်း လုၵ်း ꩢႃဝ် ဢူၼ် ယုႉ ႁိုၼ် ၽႃၼ်
- **Romanized**: `me4 thau3 kap4 luk4 saau1 on1 yu5 hɯn1 phaan1`
- **Assamese Script**: মে⁴ থাও³ কাপ্‌⁴ লুক্‌⁴ সাও¹ ওন¹ যু⁵ হুন¹ ফান¹
- **Translation**: *"An impoverished grandmother and her little granddaughter lived in a rundown shack, possessing an ancient ancestral bowl."*

**[jataka_003_s04]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၽုႉ ၶႆ ၶူင် ၽႃၼ် မႃ ၶွႈ ၶႆ
- **Romanized**: `phu5 khai1 khong1 phaan1 maa1 khɔ3 khai1`
- **Assamese Script**: ফু⁵ খাই¹ খোঙ¹ ফান¹ মা¹ খঅ³ খাই¹
- **Translation**: *"The dishonest, greedy peddler arrived; the grandmother brought out the soot-blackened bowl to trade for a cheap trinket."*

**[jataka_003_s05]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: မၼ် ၶိတ်း ႁိၼ် ႁၼ် ၽႃၼ် ၶမ် ထး
- **Romanized**: `man1 khit4 hin1 han1 phaan1 kham1 thae4`
- **Assamese Script**: মান¹ খিত্‌⁴ হিন¹ হান¹ ফান¹ খাম¹ থ⁴
- **Translation**: *"Scratching the base with a needle, the rogue discovered that the heavy black bowl was solid pure gold!"*

**[jataka_003_s06]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: မၼ် ၶိတ်း ဢဝ် ပႇ ဝႃႆႊ ဢမ် မိ ၶႃႈ
- **Romanized**: `man1 khit4 aw1 plau2 waai6 am1 mi1 khaa3`
- **Assamese Script**: মান¹ খিত্‌⁴ অৱ¹ প² ৱাই⁶ অম¹ মি¹ খা³
- **Translation**: *"Plotting to get it for nothing, he scoffed: 'This worthless old pan isn't even worth half a copper!', throwing it to the floor."*

**[jataka_003_s07]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၽုႉ ၶႆ ၶူင် ဢ ꩢႉ မႃ ထုင်ႇ ႁိုၼ်
- **Romanized**: `phu5 khai1 khong1 di1 seriva5 maa1 thung2 hɯn1`
- **Assamese Script**: ফু⁵ খাই¹ খোঙ¹ অ¹ স⁵ মা¹ থুঙ² হুন¹
- **Translation**: *"Later, the honest hawker Seriva arrived; the little girl begged her grandmother to show him the bowl."*

**[jataka_003_s08]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ꩢႉ ႁၼ် ၶမ် ဝႃႆႊ ၼႆႊ ပိၼ် ၶမ် လွႃင်
- **Romanized**: `seriva5 han1 kham1 waai6 nai6 pin1 kham1 luang1`
- **Assamese Script**: স⁵ হান¹ খাম¹ ৱাই⁶ নাই⁶ পিন¹ খাম¹ লুৱাঙ¹
- **Translation**: *"Seriva examined it, bowed reverently, and said: 'Mother, this bowl is pure gold! All my money and goods cannot equal its worth!'."*

**[jataka_003_s09]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ေမး ထဝ်ႈ ဢဝ် ၶမ် ႁိုႈ ၽုႉ ၸႆႈ ꩢႉ
- **Romanized**: `me4 thau3 aw1 kham1 heu3 phu5 tsai3 seriva5`
- **Assamese Script**: মে⁴ থাও³ অৱ¹ খাম¹ হু³ ফু⁵ চাই³ স⁵
- **Translation**: *"Overjoyed by his rare honesty, the old woman gave him the golden bowl, blessing him for his righteousness."*

**[jataka_003_s10]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၽုႉ ၽႃၼ် မႃ ထုင်ႇ ႁၼ် ၶမ် ပႆ လႈ
- **Romanized**: `phu5 phaan1 maa1 thung2 han1 kham1 pai1 liau3`
- **Assamese Script**: ফু⁵ ফান¹ মা¹ থুঙ² হান¹ খাম¹ পাই¹ ল³
- **Translation**: *"The greedy rogue returned later to snatch it, but learning it was gone, suffered a broken heart and collapsed in grief."*

**[jataka_003_s11]**
- **Lik Tai Script**: ၽႇ သဝ် ဝႃႆႊ ꩢႉ ၶိင်ႇ ၼၼ်ႊ ပိၼ် ႁဝ်
- **Romanized**: `phra2 chaw1 waai6 seriva5 khing2 nan6 pin1 haw1`
- **Assamese Script**: ফ² ছাৱ¹ ৱাই⁶ স⁵ খিঙ² নান⁶ পিন¹ হাৱ¹
- **Translation**: *"The Buddha concluded: 'The honest peddler was I; the greedy cheat was Devadatta. Honesty wins eternal glory!'."*
